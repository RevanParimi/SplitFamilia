# SF-017 — Privacy policy draft and the account-deletion question

Priority: **P0** · Task: **T-06** (story 1 of 5) · Estimate: **2 points**

Status lives in [STATE.json](../STATE.json), not in this card. Dependencies: SF-016.

Brief: [stage 5](../brief/2026-09-28-google-play-release-brief.md).

## Intended result

A draft privacy policy that says only what the app really does, with every legal or business
choice marked for the owner. A written answer to "does Play's account-deletion rule apply?" The
expected answer is no, because the app has no accounts. It also records how users can remove
their data today.

## Starting points

- The data inventory in `PLAY_CONSOLE_DECLARATIONS.md` (SF-016).
- There are no accounts: nothing imports Firebase Auth, and there is no sign-in or password. A
  group code is a shared link, not an account.
- Deletion today:
  - a person can be removed only when no expense uses them (`index.html` lines 779–786);
  - expenses can be deleted (line 798);
  - there is no way to delete a whole group or its currency setting; stretch SF-024 would add
    one.

## Acceptance criteria

- `docs/google-play/PRIVACY_POLICY.md` covers, from verified behaviour only:
  - information collected, including a statement that there is no account information;
  - expense and group information;
  - how it is used;
  - storage (Firestore; the location is OWNER CONFIRMATION);
  - transmission (TLS);
  - third parties: Firebase/Google Cloud (the database), Railway (hosting and its access logs)
    and Google Fonts;
  - analytics and crash reporting (none);
  - retention (OWNER);
  - account deletion (not applicable, with the reason);
  - data deletion (in the app today, plus on request by email);
  - security, including that the invite link grants access and should be shared like a
    password;
  - children and the target audience (consistent with SF-016);
  - changes to the policy;
  - contact.
- Placeholders are used for `<OWNER_OR_COMPANY_NAME>`, `<CONTACT_EMAIL>`, `<EFFECTIVE_DATE>` and
  `<JURISDICTION>`. Every legal choice is marked **LEGAL/OWNER INPUT REQUIRED**. There are no
  invented legal claims, such as compliance with a named law, unless the owner confirms it.
- The account-deletion analysis quotes or links Play's current account-deletion requirement
  (with the date checked) and records the conclusion.
- A "Manual/backend requirements" section says how the owner handles a deletion request in the
  Firebase console, step by step, and the response time the owner commits to (OWNER).
- The policy is consistent with SF-016. A cross-check table lists each data type in both docs.

## Required tests

- The cross-check table has no mismatch.
- Every factual sentence traces to code or to a cited external page.

## Follow-ups from T-03 (2026-09-29)

- Publishing `privacy.html` on Railway needs it in the `Dockerfile` (a `COPY` line) and in
  `.dockerignore` (a `!privacy.html` line). `npm test` checks only the files the service worker
  caches, so add a wiring assertion for `privacy.html` too, or it could 404 on Railway unnoticed.
- The Firestore rules (SF-007) and the Railway hosting (SF-011) are facts the policy can cite:
  no sign-in, groups readable by anyone with the link, no listing of groups, data in Firestore.
  The page logs at most error codes to the console, never typed text or group codes (SF-009).

## Changed by the move to Railway (2026-09-30)

The owner moved the database to Railway too, with no dependency on Firebase (T-08, T-09).

- **Storage:** the app's own server on Railway, in its region (SF-032; **OWNER CONFIRMATION
  REQUIRED**).
- **Third parties:** Railway, for hosting and the database. Firebase and Google Cloud are gone
  after SF-038. Google Fonts stays until SF-025.
- **Deleting a group's data:** a step on the Railway database, or SF-024's in-app delete. The
  Firebase console no longer holds the data.
- **The facts to cite** come from SF-033's API (no listing of groups, the code as a password,
  the rate limit), not from the Firestore rules.
- **Deleted entries (from T-08's implementation):** deleting a person or an expense in the app
  only marks it deleted in the database, so a phone sending the same change again can't bring
  it back (`server/db.js`). The text stays in the database file, and in Railway's volume
  backups, until the whole group is deleted (SF-024). The policy must say so.
